# Customer Behavior Analysis

## 1. Project Title

**StudyBuild - Project 02: Customer Behavior Analysis**

This project analyzes customer behavior for an online store using Python and a cleaned customer dataset. The goal is to turn customer data into practical business insights that can support decisions about advertising, customer retention, loyalty campaigns, discounts, and revenue growth.

---

## 2. Business Problem

The company needs to make several decisions for an upcoming management meeting:

- Which customers are the most valuable?
- Which customers may be at risk of churn?
- Which city should receive the advertising budget if only one city can be selected?
- Do discounts appear to improve purchasing behavior?
- How should customers be segmented?
- How concentrated is the company's revenue among high-value customers?
- What actions could increase sales and improve customer retention?

The analysis focuses on descriptive statistics, customer segmentation, city-level comparison, discount comparison, churn-risk identification, and Pareto revenue analysis.

---

## 3. Dataset Description

The dataset contains **58 customers** and 18 columns.

Important variables used in the analysis include:

| Variable | Description |

The dataset contains customer-level aggregated information rather than individual transaction-level records.

## 5. Analysis Steps

### 5.1 Overall Customer and Sales Analysis

The following metrics were calculated:

- Total customers
- Total purchases
- Total revenue
- Average spending per customer
- Average order value
- Return rate
- Average customer satisfaction

### 5.2 City Analysis

Cities were compared using:

- Number of customers
- Number of purchases
- Total revenue
- Average spending per customer
- Average satisfaction
- Return rate

The purpose was to identify the city with the strongest combination of commercial performance and customer behavior.

### 5.3 Loyalty Campaign Selection

A weighted loyalty score was created using four normalized variables:

- Total spending: **35%**
- Purchase count: **25%**
- Satisfaction score: **25%**
- Purchase recency: **15%**

The 10 customers with the highest composite scores were selected for the loyalty campaign.

### 5.4 Churn-Risk Analysis

A customer was classified as **At Risk** when:

```python
total_spending >= median(total_spending)
AND
last_purchase_days > 180
```

This identifies customers who historically generated relatively high value but have not purchased for more than six months.

### 5.5 Discount Analysis

Customers who used discounts were compared with customers who did not use discounts using:

- Number of customers
- Average purchase count
- Average spending
- Average satisfaction
- Return rate

The comparison was used to assess whether the dataset provides evidence that discounts increase sales.

### 5.6 Customer Segmentation

Customers were divided into four explainable groups:

- **Champions**
- **At Risk**
- **Needs Attention**
- **Other Customers**

The segmentation is based on spending, recency, and satisfaction.

### 5.7 Pareto Revenue Analysis

Customers were sorted by total spending and cumulative revenue was calculated to determine the percentage of revenue generated by the top 20% of customers.

### 5.8 CEO Dashboard Selection

Three charts were selected for a short management report:

1. Total Revenue by City
2. Customer Count by Segment
3. Pareto Revenue Concentration

These charts were chosen because they directly support decisions about advertising, retention, and revenue concentration.

## 6. Key Findings

### Overall Business Performance

The dataset contains:

- **58 customers**
- **993 purchases**
- **218,631.43** total revenue
- **3,769.51** average spending per customer
- **216.63** average order value
- **24.87%** return rate
- **2.97 / 5** average satisfaction score

The combination of a relatively high return rate and below-midpoint satisfaction indicates that the company should not rely only on customer acquisition and advertising. Improving the customer experience is also an important growth opportunity.

### Best City for Advertising: Mashhad

**Mashhad** was selected as the best city for the advertising budget.

Key metrics:

| Metric | Mashhad |
|---|---:|
| Customers | 11 |
| Purchases | 249 |
| Revenue | 64,118.44 |
| Average spending | 5,828.95 |
| Satisfaction | 2.91 / 5 |
| Return rate | 11.65% |

Mashhad generated the highest revenue and average spending while also having the lowest return rate among the analyzed cities.

However, this decision has an important limitation: the dataset does not contain advertising costs, customer acquisition source, or campaign-level outcomes. Therefore, Mashhad appears to be the strongest candidate based on existing customer behavior, but the analysis cannot directly calculate advertising ROI.

### Customers at Risk

**18 customers**, approximately **31% of the customer base**, meet the defined At Risk criteria.

The median total spending used as the value threshold was **2,128.69**.

These customers have relatively high historical spending but have not purchased for more than 180 days. This creates a meaningful retention opportunity because recovering a previously valuable customer may be more efficient than acquiring a completely new customer.

### Discount Impact

| Metric | No Discount | Discount Used |
|---|---:|---:|
| Customers | 34 | 24 |
| Avg. Purchase Count | 17.29 | 16.88 |
| Avg. Spending | 3,736.74 | 3,815.93 |
| Avg. Satisfaction | 2.76 | 3.25 |
| Return Rate | 24.66% | 25.19% |

Customers who used discounts had slightly higher average spending, but the difference is small. They also had a slightly higher return rate.

Therefore, **this dataset does not provide sufficient evidence to conclude that discounts cause higher sales**. The observed difference is descriptive and does not establish a causal relationship.

### Revenue Concentration

The top **20% of customers**, representing **12 out of 58 customers**, generated approximately **56.02% of total revenue**.

This is not a classic 80/20 pattern, but it still indicates meaningful revenue concentration. Losing several high-value customers could have a substantial effect on total revenue.

## 7. Customer Segmentation

The segmentation rules are simple and explainable.

| Segment | Rule | Customers | Main Characteristic |
|---|---|---:|---|
| **Champions** | High spending + purchase within 90 days + satisfaction ≥ 4 | 2 | Highly valuable, active, and satisfied |
| **At Risk** | High spending + more than 180 days since last purchase | 18 | Valuable customers who have become inactive |
| **Needs Attention** | Low spending + satisfaction ≤ 2 | 13 | Low-value customers with poor experience |
| **Other Customers** | All remaining customers | 25 | Core customer base with no extreme risk/value signal |

### Recommended Actions

- **Champions:** Maintain the relationship through VIP benefits, personalized offers, feedback requests, and referral programs.
- **At Risk:** Run targeted win-back campaigns and investigate the reasons for inactivity.
- **Needs Attention:** Improve customer support and investigate product or experience issues before attempting aggressive retention spending.
- **Other Customers:** Maintain standard loyalty activities and identify customers who can be developed into higher-value segments.

## 8. Business Recommendations

### Recommendation 1: Win Back High-Value Inactive Customers

**Evidence:** 18 customers, approximately 31% of the customer base, have above-median spending but have not purchased for more than six months.

**Action:** Launch a personalized win-back campaign using targeted messages, a limited-time offer, and a short feedback survey to identify reasons for inactivity.

**KPI:** Percentage of At Risk customers making at least one new purchase within 30 days. The analysis proposes a target of **15% or more**.

---

### Recommendation 2: Prioritize Mashhad for Advertising

**Evidence:** Mashhad has the highest revenue (**64,118.44**), the highest average spending (**5,828.95**), and the lowest return rate (**11.65%**).

**Action:** Prioritize the available advertising budget in Mashhad and use customer behavior in this city to guide campaign messaging.

**KPI:** Number of new customers acquired from Mashhad and their subsequent return rate.

**Risk:** Advertising ROI cannot be calculated from the current dataset because advertising cost and acquisition-source data are unavailable.

---

### Recommendation 3: Reduce the High Return Rate

**Evidence:** The overall return rate is **24.87%**, while some cities perform considerably worse. Ahvaz, for example, has a return rate of approximately **35.14%**. Overall satisfaction is also only **2.97 / 5**.

**Action:** Investigate the products and customer experiences responsible for returns. Improve product descriptions, product images, sizing information, and exchange/return processes where appropriate.

**KPI:** Reduce the overall return rate from **24.87% to below 15%** within two quarters.

## 9. Dataset Limitations

Several limitations affect the interpretation of the results:

### 1. No product-level information

The dataset does not identify which products customers purchased or returned. Therefore, the analysis cannot determine which products or categories are responsible for the high return rate.

Useful additional columns would include:

- Product ID
- Product category
- Product price
- Returned product/category
- Return reason

### 2. No marketing acquisition information

There is no information about how customers entered the business, such as advertising channel, social media, referral, or organic acquisition.

This prevents direct calculation of:

- Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC)
- Marketing channel performance
- Advertising ROI

### 3. Small sample size

The dataset contains only **58 customers**. Some cities and customer segments are therefore small, making their results sensitive to individual customers.

For example, the Champions segment contains only **2 customers**.

## 10. Conclusion

The analysis shows that the company has a meaningful customer base and generates **218,631.43** in total revenue, but there are clear opportunities to improve customer retention and customer experience.

The most important findings are:

1. **Mashhad** is the strongest candidate for advertising based on revenue, spending, and return rate.
2. **18 customers** are classified as At Risk and represent an important retention opportunity.
3. Discounts are associated with only a small difference in average spending, so the dataset does not establish that discounts cause higher sales.
4. The **24.87% return rate** and **2.97/5 satisfaction score** indicate customer-experience problems that deserve attention.
5. The top **20% of customers generate 56.02% of revenue**, creating a meaningful concentration risk.

The main business implication is that growth should not be approached only through acquiring more customers. A stronger strategy is to combine **targeted acquisition, retention of high-value customers, and improvement of the customer experience**.
